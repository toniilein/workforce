# ValOS 2027 Budget Proposal — Working Draft

**Status:** decision draft for Toni, Adi, Sven, and Lido stakeholder review  
**Prepared:** 4 September 2026  
**Proposed period:** 1 January–31 December 2027  
**Recommended core request:** **96,000 DAI**
**Assessment subsidies:** excluded pending reconciliation of the existing 60,000 DAI pool

## TL;DR

ValOS has moved from framework creation into the harder phase: operating a credible standard, maintaining its governance and content, onboarding independent assessors, and producing verified adoption evidence.

This proposal requests **96,000 DAI for 12 months** to maintain and evolve the standard, operate its governance and assurance processes, support assessor and node-operator onboarding, and report measurable progress to Lido stakeholders.

The request deliberately excludes a new assessment-subsidy pool. Lido previously disbursed **60,000 DAI for four early-adopter assessments**. Any 2027 subsidy request should follow a public reconciliation of committed, spent, and remaining funds to avoid double funding.

## Why Lido should fund the next phase

ValOS gives node operators a validator-specific operational assurance framework informed by established control approaches such as ISO 27001 and SOC 2. For Lido, the strategic value is not the existence of another document. It is a repeatable mechanism for:

1. improving operator risk management and operational resilience;
2. making operator controls easier for institutions and counterparties to evaluate;
3. creating comparable, independently reviewed evidence across operators;
4. keeping Lido involved in defining credible assurance practices for validator infrastructure; and
5. maintaining an open standard that can benefit the broader staking ecosystem.

The 2025 build phase established the governance foundations, expanded the framework, rebranded D.U.C.K. as ValOS, and designed the assurance model. The 2027 mandate should therefore be judged on operational use and evidence, not another rebranding or an open-ended content exercise.

## 2027 objectives and deliverables

### 1. Operate transparent governance

**Deliverables**

- Publish and maintain governance guidelines, decision rights, conflict-of-interest rules, assessor approval criteria, and a public decision log.
- Hold at least four documented Steering Committee cycles during 2027.
- Publish a contributor map and named maintainers for the standard, website, assurance process, and community operations.
- Maintain a public backlog with disposition of substantive issues and proposed changes.

**Acceptance evidence**

- Public governance documents and meeting/decision records.
- Public register of approved assessors and reviewed operators.
- Repository issues, pull requests, releases, and changelog.

### 2. Maintain and improve the standard

**Deliverables**

- Publish at least one stable ValOS release in 2027, with a documented change rationale and migration notes.
- Maintain mappings to relevant external control frameworks without claiming equivalence or certification.
- Resolve duplicated, ambiguous, or non-testable controls identified through operator and assessor use.
- Publish a printable specification and practical self-assessment checklist.

**Acceptance evidence**

- Versioned release, changelog, control mapping, and downloadable specification.
- Issue-level traceability between feedback and accepted/rejected changes.

### 3. Make the assurance model operational

**Deliverables**

- Maintain the assessor application, independence, approval, quality-control, and removal processes.
- Onboard at least **two approved independent assessors**, subject to qualified applicants.
- Provide standard engagement templates: scope, evidence request, findings classification, operator response, review conclusion, and public-register fields.
- Complete quality review of the first cohort and document lessons learned.

**Target outcome**

- At least **four completed independent operator assessments during 2027**, subject to assessor availability and operator participation.

The completed-assessment figure is an outcome target, not an unconditional payment trigger. Payment should be tied to the controllable process deliverables above and credible evidence of active operator engagements.

### 4. Drive useful adoption and report it honestly

**Deliverables**

- Run at least four operator onboarding or working sessions.
- Maintain an onboarding pack for operators and a separate briefing pack for institutional stakeholders.
- Publish two adoption reports: mid-year and annual.
- Report the funnel consistently: contacted, oriented, self-assessing, remediation underway, independently assessed, and publicly listed.

**Guardrail**

Do not describe self-assessed or merely participating operators as “certified.” Public communications must distinguish claimed, evidenced, and independently assessed status.

## Budget and staffing model

The 96,000 DAI request is built from **1,280 delivery hours**, equivalent to **0.62 annual FTE** using 2,080 hours as 1.0 FTE. The implied blended rate is **75 DAI/hour**.

These are role envelopes, not proposed salaries or named appointments. A single contributor may cover more than one role, but actual invoices and quarterly reporting should reconcile to these hour caps.

| Role envelope | Hours | Annual FTE | Indicative rate (DAI/h) | Cost (DAI) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Programme and governance lead | 480 | 0.23 | 75 | 36,000 |
| Standards and assurance lead | 480 | 0.23 | 80 | 38,400 |
| Adoption and community coordinator | 240 | 0.12 | 60 | 14,400 |
| Legal/editorial specialist reserve | 80 | 0.04 | 90 | 7,200 |
| **Total** | **1,280** | **0.62** | **75 blended** | **96,000** |

### Expected allocation over the year

- **Q1 — 360 hours:** governance package, annual roadmap, contributor assignments, assessor/onboarding materials.
- **Q2 — 340 hours:** standard maintenance, release candidate, assessor pipeline, operator onboarding, mid-year report.
- **Q3 — 300 hours:** assurance engagements, issue resolution, operator support, evidence and quality review.
- **Q4 — 280 hours:** stable release, annual reporting, financial reconciliation, and 2028 recommendations.

Quarterly hours may move by up to 10% without changing the annual role caps. Movement beyond that threshold should be explained in the next report and approved through the grant's milestone review.

### Budget rationale

- The prior ValOS/D.U.C.K. build phase requested **75,200 DAI for six months**. A simple annualisation would be 150,400 DAI, but carrying a build-phase run rate into steady-state operations would overfund the programme without stronger evidence of demand.
- The lean model produces a 75 DAI/hour blended rate across 1,280 hours. This should be validated against named contributors and their agreed rates before submission.
- The budget assumes a lean, part-time delivery model using the public repository and existing ValOS infrastructure. It does not include a broad paid-marketing programme.
- It does not include assessment fees or operator remediation costs funded through the existing subsidy pool.
- The hour model is intentionally **0.62 aggregate FTE**. It funds maintenance and targeted adoption rather than a full-time programme office.
- Any expansion beyond 96,000 DAI should be proposed separately after the mid-year report demonstrates assessor capacity, active operator demand, and delivery against the first two milestones.
- Until the named contributors and rates are confirmed, 96,000 DAI remains a planning envelope rather than a final priced request.

## Payment and accountability

Recommended payment structure:

- **20% — 19,200 DAI at commencement:** approved work plan, named team, public backlog, reporting template.
- **25% — 24,000 DAI after Q1:** governance package, contributor map, assessor/onboarding materials.
- **25% — 24,000 DAI after Q2:** mid-year report, evidence of assessor pipeline, stable-release candidate or documented release plan.
- **20% — 19,200 DAI after Q3:** active assessment evidence, operator sessions, resolved standard-maintenance backlog.
- **10% — 9,600 DAI after Q4:** annual report, final release/changelog, 2028 recommendations, and financial reconciliation.

Each milestone should be reviewed by the relevant Lido grant authority with input from the NOM workstream. Unused funds should be returned within 60 days after the grant period.

## Reporting dashboard

Report quarterly using a small, auditable set of indicators:

- governance decisions published;
- open/closed standard issues and pull requests;
- releases and material control changes;
- approved assessor count;
- operator funnel by stage;
- completed independent assessments;
- public reviewed-operator listings;
- budget spent, committed, and remaining; and
- risks, delays, conflicts, and departures from plan.

Counts must link to public evidence wherever confidentiality permits. Confidential engagements may be reported in aggregate, with the reason for non-disclosure stated.

## Dependencies and risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Qualified assessors do not onboard | Maintain a documented pipeline; separate assessor onboarding from completed-assessment targets. |
| Operators delay remediation or assessment | Use staged onboarding, readiness checks, and operator co-funding/skin in the game. |
| ValOS claims exceed available evidence | Enforce precise status language and publish methodology and limitations. |
| Standard maintenance becomes contributor-driven scope creep | Use an annual roadmap, issue triage, and Steering Committee approval for material changes. |
| Existing subsidy money overlaps with 2027 asks | Publish a transaction-level reconciliation before requesting new subsidies. |
| Dependence on one delivery organisation | Name maintainers, document processes, use the public repository, and establish succession/continuity arrangements. |

## Decisions required before forum submission

6. Confirm the Lido body responsible for milestone acceptance and payment authorization.
7. Decide whether any new assessment subsidies should be a separate proposal.
